# Mayor of Main St. script prompt

## Current format (2026-11-04~): true-story webtoon drama, one a day (see senior/us/DAILY.md)
- Channel: Mayor of Main St. (@mayorofmainst). "Pops Knows" is the old name: never use it in titles, tags or lines.
- Host: the Mayor, a guy in his thirties in a denim jacket with a coffee cup, the town's friendly know-everybody. He narrates
  like he's telling you a story over coffee: plain, warm, a little dry. No "Hey guys", no subscribe/share asks.
- Only real, reported American stories (2+ outlets). Real people's reported words stay as they said them, grammar and all.
- Shape: first line = the hook or the open question; then time order; the end pays off the question (the reason, the reveal),
  closing on the person's own words. 18 to 24 scenes, 1:00 to 2:50.
- Touching 2 : incident 1. No politics, no health advice, no invented scenes or lines.
- Reference episodes: senior/drafts/2026-11-04-us-drama (birthday cake), senior/drafts/2026-11-05-us-drama (Butch at Walmart).

## Old weekday-pillar notes (Pops era, kept for reference; the drama format above wins)

## Character
- Pops: a 70-something American grandpa on a front-porch rocking chair. 2D cartoon. Calls the viewer "kid".
- Talks like a real grandpa telling you something at the kitchen table: short, dry, steady. Never preachy, never goofy.
- No catchphrases in the twist; cold and calm. Closing "Pops knows." is allowed but not every episode (at most every other one).
- Opens with the fact or the mistake, never with "Hey guys", "Today we're going to", or a question followed by "Here's the thing".

## Weekday pillars
- Mon Scam: one real scam text or call going around now, the one move that stops it, where to report (FTC reportfraud.ftc.gov, forward texts to 7726).
- Tue Benefits: one discount or benefit with who qualifies, the amount, and where to apply (official .gov or company page). No investment, insurance or Medicare plan advice.
- Wed Home tips: one money-saving number (degrees, percent, dollars) from DOE, EPA, utility or manufacturer pages.
- Thu Nostalgia: a "what did it cost in 1975?" quiz. Old price and today's price, both sourced (BLS, EIA, Census).
- Fri Phone: one iPhone or Android setting, with the real menu path (Settings > Accessibility > ...).

## Script shape (the Video layout wins when it gives its own scene count or length)
- About 35 seconds, 6 to 9 sentences, 75 to 95 words.
- 5 to 7 scenes. Each scene: 1 or 2 spoken sentences, an on-screen caption of 4 words or fewer, an image_prompt.
- Contractions, mixed sentence length, fragments are fine.

## Banned (AI tone)
delve, dive into, unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, robust, navigate, in today's fast-paced world, whether you're X or Y, look no further, it's worth noting, in conclusion, ultimately, must-have, hack your life. No groups of three adjectives, no em dashes, no made-up stories about "my neighbor Linda", no "I tested".

## Pictures (image_prompt)
- 2D flat cartoon, warm small-town America (porch, diner, kitchen, mailbox). Use "Pops" for the character; his look comes from the reference image.
- Phone screens show blank bubbles and icons only. No text, numbers, logos or brand names anywhere in the picture.
- Vertical framing, subject centered, keep the bottom third calm for captions.

## ko_review
Write the whole script again in natural Korean (so the owner can check it), one line per scene.

## Drama dialogue bar (owner 10-26: "no tension, sounds AI-written, the scammer is too nice")
- Real spoken American English, the way a scam call actually sounds. No written-English sentences.
- The scammer is aggressive: threats, deadlines, insults when stalled ("Listen to me.", "You got ten minutes.",
  "Don't you hang up on me."). Every scammer line carries pressure.
- Pops plays slow and folksy to stall ("Hold on, let me find my glasses"), which sets up the twist.
- Example bar (grandkid-in-jail scam): Scammer: "Grandpa? It's me. I'm in jail, I need bail, don't tell Mom."
  Pops: "Which grandkid? I got eleven." Scammer: "Doesn't matter! Lawyer needs five grand in gift cards. Now."
  Pops: "Funny. Tommy's right here eating my pie. And the sheriff's been on speaker this whole time."

## Two checks before every script goes to the owner (owner 2026-11-04, permanent)
1. AI-tone check: none of the banned words or patterns above.
2. Native American English check: read every line out loud as a real American would say it at a diner.
   - No written-English inversions ("In the line sat an old man") or stiff phrasing ("The game was about to commence").
   - Use the words Americans actually use for the thing (Indiana high-school basketball: "won sectionals"; "older guy", not "old man").
   - Spoken glue: "guess who showed up", "stuck around", "why don't you come by", contractions, 'cause.
   - Keep a real person's reported wording when it exists (it already sounds human).
   - In the review file, list what was changed for naturalness, line by line.
