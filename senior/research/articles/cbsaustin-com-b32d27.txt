MILTON, Fla. (WEAR) — A Florida man and Vietnam veteran always knew he had a son somewhere in Thailand.
His son’s mother was pregnant when he was forced to leave the country once his deployment was up.
His son moved from Thailand to the U.S. when he was 4 years old and had been searching for his dad all this time.

Thanks to a little luck and Ancestry.com, the two were together Tuesday night for the first time.
Jim Langdon paced inside Pensacola International Airport waiting to meet someone he’s known in his heart but never met his 51-year-old son.
“She was pregnant when I left in October. He was born in January of 73'," Langdon said.
Young Jim Langdon met his Thai girlfriend, Get, in the 70s.
"I was stationed in Thailand in 1971-72. I hooked up with a young lady there. Was with her most of my tour there. We actually had plans to marry, but we waited too late," Langdon said.
The winds of fate, and the Vietnam War, forced them apart.
Get had her baby, but dad lost touch for decades until his son found him on Ancestry.com.

And a flight to Pensacola brought them together.
"Something I never thought would happen. After so many years, I just thought that there was no way to find my son. Let alone find out he’s here in the states and that his mother is still alive," Langdon said.
"When I called him, he thought I was still in Thailand. He didn’t know I spoke English," John Hermann, Langdon's son said.
Hermann moved to the U.S. with his mother and American stepfather when he was 4.
A few hours after landing in Pensacola, they were home in East Milton. It was pizza night with pops and step-mom Cathy.
Decades spent searching, sped up, thanks to Ancestry.com, and a match with his first cousin, Langdon’s niece.
A family tree, now filled out, with old photos branching from the past to the present.

Hermann hopes his story inspires other kids, still yearning to be found, to find their family and their place in the world.
"Being around him, it feels like a sense of belonging," Hermann said.
This all happened really fast, within the last 4 months or so.
Hermann only bought a one-way ticket from San Diego to Pensacola. After 51 years, goodbyes can wait.
